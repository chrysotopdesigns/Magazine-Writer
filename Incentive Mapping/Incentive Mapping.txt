1. Game Theory and Incentive Fundamentals - Builds a structured way to examine choices and competing interests.
2. Source Motivation Mapping - Identifies possible source interests as hypotheses to verify, not conclusions.
3. Stakeholder and Interest Analysis - Shows who may benefit, bear costs, or influence a decision.
4. Economic Incentives and Institutions - Connects individual choices to organizational and market structures.
5. Interview Questions and Neutral Framing - Tests incentives with fair questions rather than leading assumptions.
6. Conflicts of Interest and Disclosure - Recognizes interests that should be checked and transparently reported.
7. Power, Information, and Asymmetric Access - Explains how unequal information can shape decisions and narratives.
8. Source Reliability and Corroboration - Separates a source's possible incentive from the accuracy of their claims.
9. Narrative Manipulation and Evidence Checks - Tests framing and persuasion against independently verifiable facts.
10. Incentive Clues in Documents and Data - Uses records to substantiate claims about rewards and constraints.
11. Ethical Source Relationships and Negotiation - Maintains clear boundaries while seeking useful information.
12. Uncertainty and Limits of Game-Theoretic Models - Prevents simplified models from being mistaken for real human motives.
13. Stakeholder Maps and Visual Analysis - Makes relationships and potential interests easier to examine.
14. Fairness, Attribution, and Reporting Ethics - Avoids imputing motives without evidence and gives subjects a fair response.
15. Incentive Mapping Case Studies - Applies systematic interest analysis to documented reporting examples.
