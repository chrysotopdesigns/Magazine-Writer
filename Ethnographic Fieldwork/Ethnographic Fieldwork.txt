1. Ethnographic Principles and Informed Consent - Builds fieldwork on respect, transparency, and participant choice.
2. Community Entry and Trust Building - Plans responsible access without promising outcomes the reporter cannot control.
3. Participant Observation Methods - Records everyday practices carefully while minimizing disruption.
4. Fieldnotes, Journals, and Observation Logs - Captures observations with time, context, and clear separation from interpretation.
5. Semi-Structured Interviewing in the Field - Uses open questions to learn from participants in their own terms.
6. Reflexivity and Researcher Positionality - Examines how the reporter role and background shape what is observed.
7. Gatekeepers, Access, and Representation - Navigates access while avoiding dependence on a single powerful intermediary.
8. Community Safety, Privacy, and Confidentiality - Reduces risk to participants and handles sensitive information responsibly.
9. Cultural Humility and Translation - Works across language and cultural differences with care and consultation.
10. Observation Without Unnecessary Interference - Balances presence and documentation with respect for participants and setting.
11. Qualitative Coding and Field Data Analysis - Finds patterns in notes while preserving variation and contradiction.
12. Triangulation Across Field Evidence - Checks observations against interviews, documents, and other sources.
13. Harm Assessment and Reporting Back - Considers consequences and closes the loop with communities where appropriate.
14. Ethnographic Narrative and Evidence - Turns field experience into a clear story without overgeneralizing.
15. Fieldwork Planning and Portfolio - Prepares a safe, practical field plan and evaluates its results.
